# CASPER
Code base for paper: "CAspER in the Machine: Insights into Character Variety in LLM-Generated Stories"

Accepted to the Proceedings of ACL, 2026 (main)

*Material and code base will be committed soon.*

## Overview

In this work, we:

- Adapt narratological methods to automatically understand characters at level of portrayal;
- Construct high-quality labeled dataset of human-written & LLM-generated short stories;
- Compare and analyze LLM-generated & human-written characters.

We present CASPER as a method for analyzing 8 axes of character portrayal. Using CAspER, we address *(1) Do LLM-generated stories and human-written stories have similar characters?* and *(2)
Do LLMs generate stories with a variety of characters?* To this end, we create a corpus of 200 human-written short stories from [r/shortstories](https://www.reddit.com/r/shortstories/) and 4400 LLM-generated short stories from 8 models spanning four families (Llama, Phi, Mistral, GPT4o-mini). We perform character classification with an LLM-judge evaluated on a hand-labeled testset of 100 stories with average F1-macro score = 84.64.

## Key Takeaways

- LLMs-generated stories are more likely to “play it safe.”
- Within a model family, size does not change types of characters generated.
- Phi family generates most diverse; Llama family generates least diverse characters.
- Genre plays major role in types of character
- When re-generating stories from a prompt, literalness & closure vary most.
